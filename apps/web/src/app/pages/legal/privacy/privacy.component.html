This Privacy Policy explains what information Birthdly (birthdly.com) collects, how it is used and stored, and the choices you have. Birthdly is a birthday calendar operated by an individual developer. By using Birthdly you agree to this policy and to the Terms of Service.

Information we collect

  • Birthdays you add. The name and birth date (the birth year is optional) of each person you add to your calendar.
  • Photos of people you add. If you are signed in, you can add a photo to a person in your calendar. Before the photo leaves your device, your browser crops it to a small square image (256 × 256 pixels) and saves it again, so the original file and its metadata, such as the location where it was taken, are not uploaded. Birthdly shows the photo only to you: your account gets it through temporary links that work for up to two days, and anyone you share such a link with could open it until it expires. Photos are not sent to Google Calendar or Google Contacts and do not appear in notifications or invite links. Without an account, photos cannot be added.
  • Google account information. When you sign in with Google, we receive your Google account ID, email address, name, and profile picture. We store your account ID, email address, name, and the address of your profile picture to create your Birthdly account and to show who is signed in. The picture itself is loaded from Google and is not copied to our servers.
  • Google Calendar data. If you connect Google Calendar, Birthdly reads the birthday events in your primary calendar and creates, updates, and deletes the birthday events that Birthdly itself adds there. We store the IDs of those events so they stay in sync with your birthdays.
  • Google Contacts data. If you connect Google Calendar, Birthdly also reads your Google contacts to import their names and birthdays into your Birthdly calendar. We store only the name, the birth date, and the contact ID of contacts that have a birthday. Other contact details, such as phone numbers, email addresses, or addresses, are not read or stored. If you turn on birthday notifications, the names of imported contacts can appear in them, as described below.
  • Google refresh token. To keep syncing after you connect, we store a Google refresh token, encrypted, in our database. Only Birthdly's server uses it; it is never sent to your browser.
  • Notification settings and device subscriptions. If you turn on birthday notifications, we store when you want them (the days, the time, and your time zone) and, for each device where you turn them on, its push subscription: an address at your browser's push service and the keys that encrypt notifications for that device. We also keep, for about a week, the days we already handled your notifications for, so none is sent twice.
  • Invite links. If you create an invite link, we store its random code, whether it is turned on, and the name you choose to show on it. Anyone who opens the link sees that name.
  • Birthdays sent through an invite link. If you open someone's invite link and send your birthday, we store the name and birth date you enter (the birth year is optional) and when you sent them. You do not need an account. Only the person who shared the link sees what you sent, and they choose whether to add it to their calendar. If they turned on notifications, your name appears in a notification about the new request. To limit abuse, Birthdly counts requests from your IP address and may block them for up to an hour; the count is kept only in the server's memory, is not stored, and disappears about an hour after your last request or when the server restarts.
  • Feedback. If you send a message through the feedback form, we store its topic, the text, the email address you enter (optional), the language of the site, your browser's user agent, and when you sent it. If you are signed in, the message is also linked to your account. You do not need an account. Only Birthdly's administrators read these messages, and we use your email address only to reply to you. To limit abuse, Birthdly counts messages from your IP address in the server's memory in the same way as for invite links.
  • Usage statistics. To understand how Birthdly is used and improve it, the site counts page views and a few actions (such as signing in, adding a birthday, or turning on notifications) with Umami, an analytics tool that runs on Birthdly's own server; no third party receives this data. Each record has the page address, the referring site, your browser, operating system, device type, screen size, language, and an approximate location (country, region, and city) derived from your IP address. Your IP address itself is not stored, no cookies are used, and the statistics are not linked to your Birthdly account, your email address, or the birthdays you add.
  • Server logs. Birthdly's server records technical logs of requests (your IP address, the requested address, your browser's user agent, and the time) for security and troubleshooting. Older logs are overwritten as new ones are written.

Birthdly uses a cookie to keep you signed in, a short-lived cookie while you sign in with Google, and a cookie that remembers your language. Usage statistics do not use cookies.

How we use your information

We use your information only to provide Birthdly's features to you: to show your birthdays on the calendar, keep them in your account on every device, send you birthday notifications, sync them with your Google Calendar, import birthdays from your Google contacts, answer and act on your feedback, and see in aggregate which parts of Birthdly are used. We do not sell your information, use it for advertising, or share it with anyone except the service providers listed below that are needed to run Birthdly.

Google user data

Birthdly requests these Google permissions only when you choose to connect Google Calendar:

  • See, create, change, and delete events on Google calendars you own (calendar.events.owned): to add your Birthdly birthdays to your primary Google Calendar, keep them up to date, remove them when you disconnect if you choose to, and read existing birthday events.
  • See and download your contacts (contacts.readonly): to import the names and birthdays of your Google contacts.

Birthdly's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • Google user data is used only to provide the user-facing features described above.
  • Google user data is stored and processed only on Birthdly's own server, hosted by DigitalOcean, to run Birthdly. Requests to Birthdly pass through Cloudflare, which delivers and protects the website. It is not shared with any other third parties. If you turn on birthday notifications, the names of imported contacts who have a birthday that day or the next are sent to your own device inside a notification. The notification is encrypted for your device and passes through your browser's push service, which cannot read it.
  • Google user data is not transferred to others, except as needed to provide those features, to comply with the law, or with your consent.
  • Google user data is not used for advertising, including personalized, retargeted, or interest-based advertising.
  • No person reads your Google user data, unless you give consent or it is needed for security or to comply with the law.
  • Google user data is not used to develop, improve, or train generalized AI or machine learning models.

Where your information is stored

  • Without an account, birthdays are saved only in your browser's local storage on your device and are not sent to our servers. When you sign in, they are moved from the browser into your account.
  • With an account, your data, except photos (see below), is stored in Birthdly's own database on a server hosted by DigitalOcean in London, United Kingdom. Data is encrypted in transit (HTTPS), and Birthdly's server checks every request so that each user can reach only their own data. Google refresh tokens are encrypted (AES-256) in the database and are used only by Birthdly's server; they are never sent to your browser.
  • Photos of people you add are stored in a private storage bucket at Cloudflare R2. The bucket is not public; a photo can be opened only through a temporary link that Birthdly's server creates for your account.

Service providers

  • DigitalOcean — hosts Birthdly's server and database.
  • Cloudflare — delivers and protects the website and stores the photos of people you add (Cloudflare R2). Every request to Birthdly passes through it, so it processes your IP address and the requests.
  • Google — sign-in, Google Calendar, and Google Contacts, when you choose to use them.
  • Google Fonts — delivers the icon font, so Google receives your IP address when a page loads.
  • Wikimedia Foundation — serves the photos on the famous birthdays pages from Wikimedia Commons, so it receives your IP address when such a page shows a photo.
  • Your browser's push service, such as Google for Chrome, Mozilla for Firefox, Apple for Safari, or Microsoft for Edge — delivers birthday notifications and notifications about new birthday requests, when you turn them on. Notifications are encrypted for your device, so the push service cannot read the names in them.

Data retention and deletion

  • We keep your data while your account exists. You can delete any birthday in the app at any time.
  • Birthdly deletes a person's photo from storage when you replace or remove it and when you delete the person (including by deleting their birthday event that Birthdly added to your Google Calendar). If that deletion fails because of a technical error, the photo can no longer be opened through Birthdly and is deleted with your account. All your photos are deleted when your account is deleted.
  • Disconnecting Google Calendar in Settings revokes Birthdly's access to your Google account and deletes the stored Google token. When you disconnect, you choose whether Birthdly also removes the birthday events it added to your Google Calendar. If you choose to remove them, Birthdly deletes the stored IDs of those events and attempts to remove the events; if removing them fails, they stay in your Google Calendar, where you can delete them yourself. If you keep them, they stay in your Google Calendar without further updates, and Birthdly keeps their IDs with your birthdays, so that connecting the same Google account again updates these events instead of adding them twice.
  • Turning off birthday notifications for a device in Settings deletes its push subscription. When you sign out on a device, its subscription stops working and is deleted the next time a notification is sent.
  • Birthdays imported from Google Contacts (name, birth date, and contact ID) stay in your Birthdly account after you disconnect, until you delete them or ask us to delete your account.
  • A birthday sent through an invite link is kept until the person who shared the link adds or rejects it, or deletes their account. Once added, it is part of their calendar like any birthday they add themselves, and they can delete it at any time. To have a birthday you sent removed, ask that person, or email us at the address below. Turning an invite link off or creating a new one stops the old link from working.
  • Feedback messages are kept until we have dealt with them and no longer need them. Messages you sent while signed in are deleted with your account. To have a message removed earlier, email us at the address below.
  • You can also revoke Birthdly's access at any time on your Google Account permissions page.
  • To delete your account and all associated data, email us at birthdly.support@gmail.com. We delete it within 30 days.
  • Birthdays saved without an account can be removed by deleting them in the app or clearing your browser's site data.
